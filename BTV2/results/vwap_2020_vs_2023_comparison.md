The strategy in its current form loses money in both market periods:

### 3. Trade Count Below Target

### 4. Win Rates Too Low

## Analysis

### Why Mean Reversion Fails in Both Periods

1. **Strong Trends Persist**: Both 2020-2022 and 2023-2024 had strong trending behavior that invalidates mean reversion assumptions.

2. **Stop Loss Too Tight**: The 0.5 ATR stop gets hit frequently during trends.

3. **Target Too Ambitious**: The 2.5 ATR target requires significant price movement back to VWAP, which doesn't happen in trending markets.

4. **HTF Filters Don't Help**: The 1h VWAP and 4h EMA/ADX filters reduce trades but don't improve win rate enough to offset the losses.

### Observations

- 2020-2022 had more volatile swings (COVID crash in March 2020, bear market mid-2021), which should theoretically favor mean reversion, but the strategy still lost heavily.
- The strategy may work better in genuinely flat/ranging markets with low volatility, which were rare in both periods.

## Recommendations

1. **Strategy Inversion**: Try trend-following instead of mean reversion (entry_mode="bull_pullback" or "momentum")

2. **Wider Stops**: Increase ATR stop to 1.0-1.5 to survive trends

3. **Tighter Targets**: Reduce ATR target to 1.5-2.0 for more frequent wins

4. **Alternative Periods**: Test on 2018-2019 (more ranging bear market) or 2014-2017

5. **Different Strategy**: Consider trend-following strategies for these trending periods
