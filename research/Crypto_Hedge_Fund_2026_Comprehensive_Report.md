## 2. UAE (Dubai - VARA)

### 2.1 Regulatory Framework

**Regulator:** VARA (Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority) - the world's first independent regulator specifically for virtual assets.

**Licensing Activity:** VA Management and Investment Services - one of eight distinct licensed VA activities:
1. VA Advisory Services
2. VA Broker-Dealer Services
3. VA Custody Services
4. VA Exchange Services
5. VA Lending and Borrowing Services
6. **VA Management and Investment Services**
7. VA Transfer and Settlement Services
8. VA Issuance Category 1

**Important Note:** A VASP can apply for multiple activities under a single licence, EXCEPT Custody Services which must be a separate legal entity. Proprietary trading is prohibited under a regulated activity licence - a separate company must be established.

### 2.2 Licensing Process

**Two-stage process (confirmed by VARA):**

**Stage 1 - Approval to Incorporate (ATI):**
1. Submit Initial Disclosure Questionnaire (IDQ) to DET or a Free Zone
2. Provide additional documentation (business plan, beneficial owners, senior management details)
3. Pay initial fees (typically 50% of licence application fee)
4. Receive ATI to finalize legal incorporation and complete operational setup
5. **The firm is NOT permitted to carry on VA activities at this stage**

**Stage 2 - Full VASP Licence:**
1. Prepare and submit documentation per VARA guidance
2. Receive feedback from VARA (may include meetings, interviews, further documentation)
3. Pay remaining application fees and first year's supervision fees
4. Receive VASP Licence (may be subject to operational conditions)

**Additional Stage:** VARA also has an In-Principle Approval (IPA) stage visible on the public register, which is a conditional step allowing applicants to complete final requirements. IPA holders are strictly prohibited from initiating operations.

### 2.3 Timeline

- **Industry estimate:** 4-7 months
- **More realistic estimate:** 6-12 months for new entrants
- VARA does not publish official timelines
- Depends heavily on application completeness, business model complexity, and VARA's workload

### 2.4 Capital Requirements (Corrected from Original Report)

| VA Activity | Paid-Up Capital Requirement |
|---|---|
| Advisory Services | AED 100,000 |
| Broker-Dealer Services | AED 400,000 (with VARA custodian) / AED 600,000 (without) |
| Custody Services | Higher of AED 600,000 or 25% of fixed annual overheads |
| Exchange Services | AED 800,000 (with custodian) / AED 1,500,000 (without) |
| Lending and Borrowing | Higher of AED 500,000 or 25% of fixed annual overheads |
| **Management & Investment** | **AED 280,000** (with VARA custodian) / **AED 500,000** (without) |

Capital must be held in a UAE trust account with VARA as beneficiary. If licensed for multiple activities, requirements are cumulative.

### 2.5 Comprehensive Requirements

**Physical Office:** Required. For Management and Investment Services, a private office is required (not just a flex desk). Advisory Services is the only activity that may use a flex desk.

**Responsible Individuals:** Two (2) required, each must be:
- A full-time employee
- A Fit and Proper Person
- A resident of the UAE or holder of a UAE passport
- Notified to and approved by VARA

**AML/CTF Framework:** Compulsory rulebook. Recent developments include:
- GAP assessments under new UAE Federal Decree-Law on AML/CFT/CPF (2025)
- EOCN system registration for sanction alerts
- UAE Financial Intelligence Unit IEMS system registration
- FATF high-risk jurisdiction monitoring

**Additional Requirements:**
- Certificate of entity incorporation
- List of Ultimate Beneficial Owners (UBOs)
- Source of funds evidence
- Organizational structure and governance framework
- Regulatory business plan
- Insurance certificate(s)
- Succession plan and wind-down plan
- Technology and information security compliance
- Market conduct compliance
- ESG disclosure requirements

### 2.6 Tax Treatment (Corrected)

**Personal Income Tax:** 0% - No personal income tax on employment income, investment income, or capital gains for individuals.

**Corporate Tax (Critical Nuance):**
- UAE introduced a **9% federal corporate tax** effective for financial years starting on or after 1 June 2023
- Standard rate: 9% on taxable income exceeding AED 375,000
- 0% rate: On taxable income up to AED 375,000
- Free Zone entities can benefit from 0% on "Qualifying Income" but must meet strict conditions:
  - Maintain adequate substance in the UAE
  - Derive "Qualifying Income" (transactions with other Free Zone persons or certain qualifying activities)
  - Comply with transfer pricing documentation requirements
- **Fund management fees from non-Free Zone clients are likely taxable at 9%**
- Qualifying Investment Fund exemptions exist under Cabinet Decision No. 81 of 2023

**CARF Implementation:** Public consultation on UAE's implementation of the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) began October 2025, signaling upcoming international tax reporting obligations.

## 3. United Kingdom (FCA)

### 3.1 Regulatory Framework

**Regulator:** FCA (Financial Conduct Authority) under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000

**Legislative Instrument:** The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/102), made by Parliament on 4 February 2026.

### 3.2 Timeline & Phases

#### Phase 1: Pre-regime (2019-2023)
- Jan 2020: FCA becomes AML/CTF supervisor for crypto firms under MLRs
- ~85% application rejection/withdrawal rate during MLR registration

#### Phase 2: Financial Promotions & Travel Rule (2023)
- Sep 1, 2023: Travel Rule takes effect
- Oct 8, 2023: Cryptoasset financial promotions regime goes live
- FCA issued 146 alerts on day one for non-compliant promotions

#### Phase 3: Policy Development (2024-2025)
- May 2024: FCA relaxes ban on crypto ETNs for professional investors
- Throughout 2025: 12+ consultation and discussion papers published
  - CP25/14: Stablecoin issuance and custody
  - CP25/15: First prudential regime consultation
  - CP25/25: Application of FCA Handbook
  - CP25/40: Regulating cryptoasset activities
  - CP25/41: Admissions, disclosures, market abuse
  - CP25/42: Extended prudential regime

#### Phase 4: Implementation (2026-2027)
| Date | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| Jan 2026 | CARF (Cryptoasset Reporting Framework) takes effect for tax reporting |
| Feb 2026 | SI 2026/102 made by Parliament |
| Feb 2026 | GC26/2: Consumer Duty guidance for crypto firms |
| **Sep 30, 2026** | **Application gateway opens** |
| Feb 28, 2027 | Application gateway closes |
| **Oct 25, 2027** | **Full FSMA crypto regime commences** |

### 3.3 Current Interim Regime (MLR Registration)

| Aspect | MLR Registration (Current) | Full FSMA (From Oct 2027) |
|--------|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| Activities | Exchange services, custodian wallets | Full range: platforms, staking, dealing, custody, stablecoins |

### 3.4 Full Authorisation Requirements

1. **Part 4A Permissions** (FSMA authorisation for each regulated activity)
2. **SM&CR** (Senior Managers & Certification Regime):
   - Key SMFs: CEO (SMF1), Finance (SMF2), Compliance (SMF16), MLRO (SMF17)
   - Statements of Responsibilities for each senior manager
   - Annual certification of employees in risk-sensitive roles
3. **Consumer Duty** (GC26/2 provides crypto-specific guidance):
   - Products designed for identified target markets
   - Fair value assessment
   - Clear communications
   - Adequate consumer support
   - Primarily applies to retail customers
4. **Prudential Standards** (CP25/15, CP25/42):
   - Minimum capital requirements (TBD in final rules)
   - Own funds and liquidity requirements
   - Wind-down planning
5. **Market Abuse Regime** (CP25/41)
6. **Admissions & Disclosures** (CP25/41)

## 4. Cayman Islands (CIMA)

### 4.1 Regulatory Framework

**Regulator:** CIMA (Cayman Islands Monetary Authority)

**Key Legislation:**
- Mutual Funds Act (Revised) - for open-ended funds
- Private Funds Act (2020) - for closed-ended funds
- Securities Investment Business Act (SIBA) - for investment managers
- Virtual Asset (Service Providers) Act (2020, amended 2024) - for VASP activities

### 4.2 Fund Statistics (Q4 2025)

|---|---|
| Registered Mutual Funds | 12,876 |
| Private Funds | 17,722 |
| **Total Funds** | **30,598** |
| Registered VASPs | 18 |

The Cayman Islands hosts an estimated **60-70% of the world's hedge fund assets**, making it the undisputed global leader for fund domiciliation.

### 4.3 Tax Treatment

- **0% income tax** (no personal income tax)
- **0% capital gains tax**
- **0% corporate/profits tax**
- **0% withholding tax**
- **0% inheritance/estate tax**

**Nuances:**
- Annual government fees for registered entities
- Import duties (22-27% on goods)
- OECD Pillar Two (15% minimum effective tax for large MNEs with revenue >EUR 750M) may apply to very large groups
- CARF implementation expected

### 4.4 Fund Registration Categories

| Category | Min. Investment | Key Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **Registered Fund** (most common) | US$100,000 per investor | CIMA-registered administrator; annual audit |
| Administered Fund | None | Licensed Cayman administrator |
| Licensed Fund | None | CIMA approves operators; fit & proper |
| Master Fund | N/A | Registered if feeder is regulated |
| Limited Investor Fund | Max 15 investors | Simplified registration |
| Private Fund | N/A | Closed-end; 17,722 registered |

### 4.5 VASP Regime (Major 2025 Development)

**Critical milestone: April 1, 2025 - Full VASP licensing commenced**

- Virtual asset custody and trading platform services now require a full VASP licence (not just registration)
- Existing VASPs had 90 days to apply for a licence
- New entrants must obtain a licence before commencing operations

**Key distinction:** A Cayman fund that merely *invests in* virtual assets is regulated under the Mutual Funds Act or Private Funds Act - NOT the VASP Act. The VASP Act targets entities that *provide VA services to others*.

If the fund or manager operates a trading platform, provides custody, issues VAs, or provides exchange services, VASP licensing IS required in addition to fund regulation.

### 4.6 Common Fund Structures

#### 1. Master-Feeder (Most Common for Institutional Crypto)
- Cayman Master Fund holds all investments
- Cayman Offshore Feeder for non-US tax-exempt investors
- US Domestic Feeder (Delaware LP) for US taxable investors

#### 2. Segregated Portfolio Company (SPC)
- Single entity with multiple segregated portfolios
- Assets/liabilities legally ring-fenced per portfolio
- Cost-efficient for multi-strategy funds

#### 3. Standalone (Cayman Exempted Company)
- Simplest structure
- Most cost-effective for single-strategy funds

#### 4. Limited Partnership (ELP)
- Common for venture/PE-style crypto funds

### 4.7 Economic Substance Requirements

**For the fund vehicle:** Reduced requirements - maintain registered office, adequate records, file annual notifications.

**For a Cayman-based investment manager:** Must demonstrate adequate substance:
- CIGA directed and managed in Cayman
- Adequate employees, expenditure, and physical presence
- Decision-making in Cayman

**This is why most crypto funds domicile the fund in Cayman but locate the investment manager in Singapore, UAE, US, or UK.**

### 4.8 AML/CFT & FATF Status

- Full AML/CFT framework under Proceeds of Crime Act and AML Regulations
- Travel Rule compliance effective July 1, 2022
- **Removed from FATF grey list in June 2023** (placed February 2021)
- MLRO appointment mandatory
- CDD, EDD, SAR reporting, record keeping, risk assessment, staff training all required

## 5. Switzerland (FINMA)

### 5.1 Regulatory Framework

**Regulator:** FINMA (Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority)

**Key Legislation:**
- Financial Institutions Act (FinIA) - effective 1 January 2020
- Financial Services Act (FinSA) - effective 1 January 2020
- Collective Investment Schemes Act (CISA)
- Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA)
- DLT Act - fully in force 1 August 2021

### 5.2 Licence Types

| Licence Type | Min. Capital | Key Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **Manager of Collective Assets** | CHF 200,000 + collateral | Commercial enterprise; manage 1+ CIS |
| **Fund Management Company** | CHF 1,000,000+ | Swiss AG; 3+ board members; registered shares |
| Portfolio Manager | CHF 100,000 | Supervised by SO, not directly by FINMA |
| FinTech Licence | CHF 300,000 | Accept deposits up to CHF 100M; can custody crypto |
| Banking Licence | CHF 10,000,000+ | Full banking operations |
| DLT Trading Facility | Varies | For DLT securities trading platforms |

**De Minimis Exception:** An institution may qualify as a mere "portfolio manager" if:
- AUM of qualified-investor CIS does not exceed CHF 100M (including leverage), OR
- AUM does not exceed CHF 500M for non-leveraged funds where investors cannot redeem for 5 years

### 5.3 The L-QIF (Game-Changer Since March 2024)

The **Limited Qualified Investor Fund (L-QIF)**, effective 1 March 2024, is a major development:

- **No FINMA product approval required** - the fund itself is exempt
- **Faster time-to-market** - eliminates lengthy FINMA product approval
- **Restricted to qualified investors** only
- Fund manager must be FINMA-supervised (providing "indirect supervision")
- Can take any Swiss fund form: contractual fund, SICAV, LP, SICAF
- Modelled after Luxembourg's RAIF

**This significantly improves Switzerland's competitiveness for crypto fund launches.**

### 5.4 Swiss DLT Act

Fully in force since 1 August 2021, the DLT Act:
- Created **DLT Securities (Registerwertrechte)** with full legal recognition
- Introduced **DLT Trading Facility** licence category
- Established **bankruptcy segregation** for crypto-based assets in custody
- Amended 10 existing federal acts (technology-neutral approach)

**Impact:** Provides legal certainty for tokenized fund units, enables tokenized fund shares, and gives institutional investors confidence in Swiss custody.

- Contractual funds (FCP) are generally tax-transparent

## 6. Singapore (MAS)

### 6.1 Regulatory Framework

**Regulator:** MAS (Monetary Authority of Singapore) - integrated financial regulator and central bank

**Key Legislation:**
- Payment Services Act 2019 (PS Act) - for DPT services
- Securities and Futures Act 2001 (SFA) - for fund management
- Variable Capital Companies Act 2018 - for fund vehicles

### 6.2 Licensing Framework for Crypto Hedge Funds

A crypto hedge fund typically needs a **CMS License for Fund Management** under the SFA:

| License Type | Description | Min. Capital |
|---|---|---|
| **LFMC (A/I)** | Accredited/institutional investors only | S$250,000 |
| LFMC (Retail) | Can manage for retail investors | S$1,000,000 |
| RFMC | Lighter regime; max 30 investors, AUM <S$250M | S$250,000 |
| VCFM | Venture capital funds only | Simplified |

**Processing Time:** Up to 6 months for complete applications. Practical reality for crypto: **9-18 months.**

**If the fund manager directly handles DPTs** (rather than using third-party exchanges), a separate **Major Payment Institution (MPI)** licence under the PS Act may be needed (S$250,000 base capital).

### 6.3 Variable Capital Company (VCC) Structure

The VCC (available since January 2020) is the optimal fund vehicle:

- **Sub-fund segregation:** Each sub-fund's assets/liabilities legally ring-fenced
- **Variable capital:** Issue/redeem shares without shareholder approval
- **Tax efficiency:** Eligible for Section 13O/13U exemptions
- **Confidentiality:** Register of members not publicly available
- **Re-domiciliation:** Foreign funds can transfer registration to Singapore as a VCC
- **Multi-strategy:** One VCC can house multiple strategies as separate sub-funds

**Requirements:** At least one Singapore-resident director, registered office in Singapore, MAS-licensed fund manager, appointed auditor.

### 6.4 Tax Framework (Corrected)

**Corporate Tax:** 17% flat rate (effective ~8.3% on first S$200,000 due to partial exemption).

**No Capital Gains Tax** - but this is misleading for funds. Active trading profits of a crypto hedge fund are likely treated as **taxable income at 17%**.

## 8. Strategy Framework

### 8.1 Multi-Strategy Quantitative Model

The dominant institutional approach, employing multiple uncorrelated alpha sources:

#### Arbitrage Strategies
- **Cross-exchange arbitrage:** Spreads compressed but viable for new listings, illiquid pairs, CEX-DEX arb
- **CeFi-DeFi rate arbitrage:** Significant rate differentials between on-chain and OTC lending markets
- **Basis trade / Cash-and-carry:** Buy spot, short perpetual futures, earn funding rate spread
  - Annualized basis typically 5-15% in normal markets, can spike to 30%+
  - Ethena's sUSDe industrialized this at scale (7-12% APY as of 2025)
  - Returns compressed from 50%+ as more capital entered
  - Key risk: Funding can go negative in bear markets
- **Cross-chain arbitrage:** Exploit price differences across different L1/L2 chains

#### Momentum / Trend-Following
- Crypto markets exhibit strong momentum characteristics
- Trend-following historically captures 60-80% of major crypto moves
- Requires regime detection (bull/bear/sideways behave very differently)

#### Yield Strategies (Current Yields)
| Source | Yield Range | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|
| Staked stablecoins (sUSDe, sDAI) | 4-12% | Medium |
| Native staking (ETH) | ~3.0-3.5% | Low |
| Native staking (SOL) | ~7.3% | Low-Medium |
| Lending (USDC on Aave) | 3-8% | Medium |
| Tokenized Treasuries (BUIDL, BENJI) | 4-5% | Low |
| Liquid staking (stETH, JitoSOL) | 3-7.5% | Medium |

#### Emerging Strategies
- **MEV (Maximal Extractable Value):** Sandwich attacks, DEX arb, liquidation front-running
- **Liquidation strategies:** Participating in on-chain liquidation events
- **Event-driven:** Protocol upgrades, airdrops, token unlocks, regulatory events
- **Funding rate harvesting:** Distinct from basis trade; pure funding rate capture

### 8.2 Market-Neutral Overlays with Volatility Targeting

- Bitcoin annualized volatility: typically 50-80%
- Institutional crypto funds target 10-20% annualized portfolio volatility
- Dynamic hedging with options (puts, collars) and futures
- Ethena's USDe is a delta-neutral synthetic dollar (long spot + matching short perps)
- Deribit handles >$1B daily notional in BTC/ETH options

### 8.3 Gold Futures Allocation (5-10%)

**Assessment:** Reasonable but not the only or necessarily best hedge.

**Bitcoin-Gold Correlation:**
- Long-term average: ~0.1-0.2 (low correlation = genuine diversification)
- Risk-off events: Near zero or slightly negative (good hedge)
- Inflationary periods: Positive (~0.3-0.5)
- Crypto-specific crises: Uncorrelated (genuine tail-risk protection)

**Alternative Hedges to Consider:**
- Long-dated BTC put options (direct tail-risk protection)
- Short-term Treasury bills (cash buffer, ~4-5% yield)
- VIX-linked instruments (equity vol as risk-off proxy)
- 10-20% non-crypto allocation may be more appropriate for comprehensive hedging

### 8.4 Performance Targets (Adjusted)

**Original Claims vs. Realistic Assessment:**

| Metric | Original Target | Adjusted Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Sharpe Ratio | > 1.2 | > 1.2 (market-neutral book); > 0.8 (blended portfolio) |
| Max Drawdown | < 25% | < 25% (normal conditions); stress scenarios may exceed |

**Context:**
- Bitcoin long-term Sharpe: ~0.8-1.0; can exceed 2.0 in bull markets
- Basis trade Sharpe: 1.5-3.0 in favorable funding environments
- Bitcoin drew down ~40% in 2025-2026; -83% in 2018; -73% in 2022
- Max DD < 25% requires >50% market-neutral allocation and active hedging
- **Include Conditional VaR (CVaR) as additional risk metric**

### 8.5 Crypto Hedge Fund Industry Performance (2024-2025)

- Directional/long-biased: ~40-80% returns in 2024 (BTC +120%)
- Market-neutral/quant: ~10-25% returns in 2024
- Significant losses for directional funds in late 2025/early 2026 (BTC drawdown nearing 40%)

## 12. Counterparty Risk Management (Post-FTX)

### 12.1 Lessons from 2022 Collapse

The 2022 crypto lending market collapse destroyed the largest CeFi lenders:
- Genesis ($14.6B loan book), Celsius, BlockFi, Voyager
- Combined 40% of the entire crypto lending market
- CeFi lending market collapsed 82%

**Root Causes Identified:**
- Poor asset-liability management (lending long, borrowing short)
- Unsecured/undercollateralized lending
- Acceptance of toxic collateral (stETH at discount, GBTC at discount, illiquid ASICs)
- Poor internal risk controls

### 12.2 Post-FTX Industry Improvements

- **Off-exchange settlement** (Copper ClearLoop, Fireblocks) reduces exchange counterparty risk
- **Overcollateralized lending** becoming standard
- **DeFi lending share** grew from 34% to 63% of total market (algorithmic, transparent risk management)
- **Consolidation** around better-managed CeFi entities (top 3 hold 89% market share)
- **Crypto lending market recovery** to $36.5B by Q4 2024 (DeFi lending apps +959% from bear market lows)

### 12.3 Counterparty Risk Framework for New Fund

1. **Exchange exposure limits:** Max 20% of NAV on any single exchange
2. **Off-exchange settlement:** Use ClearLoop/Fireblocks where available
3. **Custody segregation:** Assets held in qualified custody, not on exchange
4. **Daily reconciliation:** Verify all positions across all venues
5. **Counterparty credit assessment:** Regular review of exchange/lender solvency
6. **Diversified execution venues:** Spread across 3-5+ exchanges
7. **Real-time monitoring:** Automated alerts for unusual exchange behavior
8. **Stress testing:** Model simultaneous failure of largest counterparty

## 18. Risk Disclosure

Cryptocurrency and hedge fund strategies involve significant risks including but not limited to:

- **Market Risk:** Extreme volatility (Bitcoin has experienced drawdowns of 40-83% from peak)
- **Liquidity Risk:** Crypto markets can become illiquid during stress events; validator exit queues, DEX pool imbalances
- **Counterparty Risk:** Exchange failures, custodian insolvency, prime broker default (as demonstrated by FTX, Genesis, Celsius)
- **Regulatory Risk:** Rapidly evolving regulations across multiple jurisdictions; potential for adverse regulatory changes
- **Technology Risk:** Smart contract exploits, oracle manipulation, MEV attacks, infrastructure failures
- **Operational Risk:** Key person dependency, system failures, human error
- **Tax Risk:** Changing tax treatment of cryptoassets; CARF implementation; OECD Pillar Two
- **Concentration Risk:** Digital asset markets dominated by BTC and ETH; limited diversification
- **Capital Loss:** Potential for total loss of invested capital

**Past performance is not indicative of future results. Professional legal, tax, and compliance advice is essential before proceeding.**
